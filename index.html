01 / MYABROADMDS

A website that evolved with the business

The redesign was not a single before-and-after project. As MyAbroadMDs expanded its services, I continued revisiting the website to improve how information was organized, how users moved through the experience, and how the brand was communicated visually.

Inconsistent brand experience

Imagery, typography, and page structure lacked a cohesive visual system.

Unclear user journey

Establishing a clearer direction

The first redesign focused on creating a cleaner visual system and stronger hierarchy. SIP SOBER

Zero-proof discovery, built from the ground up

I created Sip Sober, a consumer iOS app for discovering zero-proof drinks and venues in New York City. I led the product from concept through design and development, building the experience in SwiftUI and Firebase while continually refining its structure, features, and visual system.

01 / SIP SOBER

From prototype to iOS app

Sip Sober began as a simple web-based map. Building the first version helped me validate the core discovery experience, but it also revealed an opportunity to create something more useful: a native app where users could explore venues, filter results, save places, and discover zero-proof options beyond the map itself.

EARLY WEB PROTOTYPE

The first version used a browser-based map to test the core idea of helping users find venues serving zero-proof drinks.

02 / SIP SOBER

Building the app experience

The first native version established the core structure of the app around three areas: Home, Map, and Favorites. This gave me a foundation for testing how users could move between account content, venue discovery, and saved places before expanding the experience further.

03 / SIP SOBER

Rethinking the app structure

As Sip Sober grew, I reconsidered how the three main areas of the app should work together. Home evolved into Discover, creating a dedicated space for exploring venues and curated content. Favorites expanded into a broader Profile experience that also included saved places, while the Map remained central to venue discovery.
